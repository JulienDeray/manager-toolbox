# Semester review: chat-tool sweep (Phase 2.5)

The full procedure behind Phase 2.5 of [`semester-review`](../SKILL.md).

After Checkpoint 1, offer a sweep of your chat tool when themes are thin on evidence or a strength
or growth area needs concrete examples. Input is the thin-evidence list and confirmed themes; this
is triangulation, **not a trawl**: sample 3-5 threads per hypothesis, then update the triangulation
table before Phase 3.

Signal categories to sample:
1. **Under pressure** (incident and on-call channels): clarity of status updates, a clean "I don't
   know yet", escalation quality, whether threads get closure or trail off
2. **Helping others** (team and help channels): do they engage with the problem and teach, or
   one-line and approve?
3. **Disagreement** (RFC and design threads): how they push back and receive pushback
4. **Organic peer sentiment**: mentions of the person by others ("thanks @X", "still waiting on @X")
5. **Broadcast writing** (change and maintenance announcements): audience-appropriate, actionable,
   on time

Guardrails:
- The sweep covers **shared team channels only** (incident, help, RFC, announcement channels). DMs
  and private channels are out of scope for review evidence, even where your own account can read
  them. The sweep stays hypothesis-driven: it checks specific claims that need evidence, it is not a
  general audit of someone's communications.
- **Quality over volume**: low chat activity is not a finding; many strong engineers communicate in
  reviews, docs, and calls. Only observed behavior counts, never absence of it.
- Findings become dated behavioral examples ("during the <date> incident, the status thread went
  quiet for 4 hours"), never quoted teammates.
- Anything material that never appeared in a 1:1 gets the feedback-gap flag, per the no-surprises
  principle.
